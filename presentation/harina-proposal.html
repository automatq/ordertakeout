Where the money goes today

Three leaks, one fix

Party trays are your highest-ticket product — $20 to $65 each. That's exactly where a percentage-based commission hurts most, and exactly where manual order-taking costs the most staff time.

1 · Commission

You already partner with Uber Eats and DoorDash. Every order that flows through them is revenue you generated, taxed at 15–30%. On a $45 tray that's $6.75–$13.50 gone before flour.

2 · Staff time

Every phone order takes roughly 5 minutes — answer, write it down, confirm the date, chase payment. During service, that's your busiest person standing still.

3 · Anonymous customers

Every walk-in leaves without a trace — no email, no history, no way to bring them back. Calls after 9 PM never happen at all, and uncollected trays are baked for nothing.

The platforms own your customer, too. On a marketplace, the customer's email, order history and reorder habit belong to the platform — not to you. Direct ordering makes that list yours, permanently.

Built to your brief

Your nine questions, answered in code

Your requirements document asked nine specific questions. Each one is a working, tested feature — not a roadmap item.

✓ 1. How will we know an order came in?Six ways at once: Square POS, staff dashboard with an audible chime, email, text message, plus Discord, Slack or Trello if you want them.
✓ 2. Does a new order notify automatically?Yes. The kitchen screen updates live and chimes. No refreshing, no checking.
✓ 3. How do staff know it's paid?Every order is paid before it's confirmed. A PAID badge and the Square payment ID sit on every card. Unpaid carts never reach the kitchen.
✓ 4. Separate order statuses?New → Paid → Preparing → Ready for Pickup → Completed, plus Cancelled. One tap each, and it mirrors into Square.
✓ 5. Where do orders appear?Dashboard, email, text, and Square POS — and the dashboard works on a phone behind the counter.
✓ 6. Notification on every paid order?Email and text fire the moment payment clears — never on an abandoned cart.
✓ 7. Can customers choose pickup date and time?Required before the pay button will even enable. No order arrives without a slot.
✓ 8. Block Ensaymada orders after the 6 PM cutoff?Enforced on the server in your store's local time. Past cutoff, tomorrow disappears from the calendar automatically.
✓ 9. Cap orders per pickup time?Per-slot caps and per-day production limits, both editable by staff. Full slots grey out. The kitchen can never be over-committed.

Why this won't break on a Saturday

Built like payment software, because it is

0known defects at handover

Money can't go wrong quietly

Every payment carries a one-time key, so a double-click or a flaky connection can't charge twice. If a payment result is ever ambiguous, the system recovers it automatically instead of guessing.

Tested against real conditions

The scheduling and capacity logic is verified against a real database with concurrent customers competing for the last slot — and every test runs in a deliberately hostile timezone to catch date bugs.

Keeps working when things fail

If Square goes down, the kitchen screen keeps working and reconciles afterwards. If an email fails, it retries on its own. A notification problem can never fail a payment that already succeeded.
